## Problem Statement

vuldesk.com fails the 5-second test for the readers it attracts.

- A real client read the site and could not tell what the company is about (founder
  report, this session).
- Copy audit: **212 confirmed jargon terms** for a non-technical reader (269 flagged,
  57 rejected by an adversarial pass, 25 more added by a completeness pass);
  74 unclear sentences; sentence mechanics measure grade 5.5 but functional difficulty
  is college-plus because comprehension rests on developer vocabulary (pull request,
  staging, eval set, CRUD, model provider keys).
- Three cold-read personas (clinic owner, home-buying president, ops manager) scored
  4/10, 6/10, 4/10 confidence on "what does this company do." All three leaned toward
  the foreclosure proof; all three asked "do you work with a business like mine, or
  only software companies?" The page answered: only software companies.
- The blog still describes a security firm (three pentest-era posts) while every CTA
  sells AI work. Two businesses on one site.
- Above the fold, "for whom" is never stated.

## Status Quo

The current live site (deployed at HEAD `3dc42f1`) sells "AI agent features, built and
handed over" to software-product companies in 8 dense sections. Its own buyers cannot
buy it: the offer mechanics (repo branch, staging, PR review, model keys) require a
dev team the new target reader does not have.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- **Reader (D2):** a business owner or operator with no programmers — clinic owner,
  ops manager, small-company president. Chosen over the Aug 9 ICP because every reader
  who ever reacted to this site is one, and the founder's own directive describes them.
- **Offer (D3):** a done-for-you AI tool: "You have a pile of records. I build a tool
  that reads them and gives you answers. You open it in your web browser. One fixed
  price." Buyable with zero technical staff.
- **Hero shape (D7, revised by founder):** the headline states the offer in plain
  words; the flagship (foreclosure tool) appears one beat later as "Real example" and
  as a proof card. The founder rejected the flagship-as-headline draft ("the heading
  text itself is confusing? dont you think?") and the 5-second retest proved the
  revision right: 3/3 fresh cold readers at 8/10 confidence (previous copy: 4-6/10).
- **Voice (D9):** person voice, company wrapper. Copy speaks as "I" (Himal, named,
  with face and city); Vuldesk Technologies Private Limited appears in the footer,
  legal pages, and contracts. Plural "we" rejected: it is the recognizable signature
  of one-person-agency cosplay in a market where 3 in 10 buyers arrive burned.

## Constraints

- Solo operator; every hour on the site is an hour not on outbound.
- Astro (AstroWind-derived); GitHub Pages deploys on push to `main`; no form backend
  (contact is mailto + calendar link today).
- Hard timebox (P5): the rewrite ships within two working days of starting, or it
  stops and ships as-is.

## Premises (all confirmed by founder; P2 revised after cross-model challenge)

- **P1.** One reader: a business owner/operator with no programmers.
- **P2 (revised).** One offer, flagship-led on the page but plain-stated in the
  headline: named example + "I build tools like this," with published fixed prices
  and named live proof.
- **P3.** Copy standard: grade 5-7 plain English, homepage ≤ ~450 words, passes the
  5-second test (bar: 80% of cold readers can say what the company does). The three
  security blog posts are unlisted, not deleted.
- **P4.** Position against the category: durability over speed ("still running after
  20 months"), published fixed prices over "book a call", plain words over outcome
  jargon, one named proof over logo bars. Evidence: grade 5-7 copy converts 11.1% vs
  5.3% (41k landing pages); jargon reduces persuasion even when defined (Shulman
  2020); the category's speed framing matches the burned buyer's #1 trigger
  (fast delivery that breaks in 90 days).
- **P5.** The site validates referrals and outbound and clears solo-operator
  objections (name/face, continuity, ownership, data handling); it is not a demand
  engine (only 9% of B2B buyers treat vendor sites as reliable; word of mouth 42% vs
  ads 2%). Site work is timeboxed; outbound outranks it.

**Page structure and copy skeleton (v2, founder-revised):**

1. **HERO** — brand line VULDESK.
   H1: "I build AI tools that read your records and give you answers."
   Sub: "Orders, cases, claims, forms — every business has a pile that someone reads
   by hand. I build a tool that reads yours and tells you what matters. You open it
   in your web browser. A price fixed before we start. No programmers needed on
   your side."
   Small line: "Real example: one of my tools read 118 foreclosure court cases and
   found the 57 that no longer mattered. 37 seconds per case."
   Button: "Tell me about your pile →" — target: the existing `CONTACT_MAILTO`
   draft (`contact@vuldesk.com`, src/navigation.js:8-13), rewritten in plain words
   end to end: subject "My pile of records"; body "What the pile is: / What I want
   to know from it: / Roughly how many per week:". The jargon gate (success
   criterion 1) covers this template — today's body says "feature" and "Stack",
   both inventory terms, and the buyer meets it one click after the hero.
2. **PROOF** — two cards: "Still running after 20 months" (IVF clinic booking system,
   2024, See it live) and "The foreclosure tool" (118 cases, 37 seconds each, See a
   sample report).
3. **PRICES** — three published tiers (amounts are placeholders from market bands;
   Open Question 3): One small tool $7,500 / A bigger tool — fixed quote, typically
   from $15,000 / Keep it healthy $500/month, optional. Tagline: "The price I quote
   is the price you pay."
4. **TRUST — "Questions a careful buyer asks"** — photo, "I am Himal. I run Vuldesk
   from Kathmandu, and I build every tool myself." Q&As: Who owns the tool? / What if
   you disappear? / What happens to my data? / How do we talk? / **What happens when
   the tool is wrong?** (added; see punch list).
5. **CLOSE** — "Have a pile of records that eats your team's time?" + Email button
   (same mailto target as the hero button; contact is mailto-only today — a
   phone/call option is Open Question 6).
   Footer: Vuldesk Technologies Private Limited · Kathmandu · Terms · Privacy
   (both legal pages exist at src/pages/terms.md and src/pages/privacy.md; they
   stay unchanged — lawyer review is already tracked in TODOS.md, out of scope).

**Copy punch list from the v2 cold-read verification (all are copy edits):**

1. Replace "I never use them to train anything" → "I never feed your records into
   anything else. They are used for your tool and nothing more." (all 3 readers
   stumbled on "train")
2. Add the fifth trust Q&A: what happens when the tool is wrong — mistakes get
   caught, a person stays in the loop, nothing irreversible happens automatically.
   (2 of 3 readers asked unprompted; matches the market's #1 burn pattern)
3. Replace "Everything lives in your accounts, not mine" with: "The tool runs under
   your business's own logins, not mine. If I disappear, it keeps working and
   nothing needs to move."
4. Reframe "Any programmer can pick up where I left off" around the buyer: "You lose
   nothing. The tool keeps running as it is, and the code and passwords are yours."
5. Any proof link that appears on day one must resolve — two readers said a
   checkable claim that does not resolve is worse than no claim. An unready proof
   element is removed before ship, not left dangling (success criterion 4).
6. In the hero sub, "One fixed price." → "A price fixed before we start." and the
   middle price tier → "fixed quote, typically from $15,000" (the tested v2 file
   still carries the older wording; the skeleton above is authoritative).

## Success Criteria

In-timebox gates (block the ship):

1. **Zero unglossed terms from `docs/designs/assets/jargon-inventory.md` in the
   final copy.** This is the primary language gate. Flesch-Kincaid grade ≤ 7 is a
   secondary check only — the current failing page measures grade 5.5 on that
   metric, so sentence mechanics alone cannot detect the vocabulary failure.
2. Homepage ≤ 450 words of user-visible copy.
3. Synthetic 5-second retest (re-run the same 3-persona cold-read harness used on
   v2) on the final copy: 3/3 state what the company does from the first screen.
4. **Every proof link that appears on the shipped page resolves on production.** A
   proof element whose target is not ready (Open Questions 2-4) is removed before
   ship, never left dangling.
5. Shipped within the two-working-day timebox with zero missed outbound days caused
   by site work.

Post-ship measures (outside the timebox):

6. The confused client, re-sent the new page, can restate the offer in their own
   words — the live test that started this session.
7. 5-second test with 3 real non-technical humans (acquaintances or clients):
   ≥ 80% state what the company does from the first screen.
